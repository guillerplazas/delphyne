# ACE roles: what improved and what still fails

The v1 curator/reducer interface improves the small writer pilot, but the
full adaptation still preferentially retains examples of existing training
successes. Training compared 29/40 at $0.62570194 with a historical flagship
28/40 at $0.68922336; this is inconclusive and is not a fresh paired verdict.
Guille stopped further benchmarking to prioritize the platform criticisms
below. Validation is administratively censored at 55/160 result files and
has no comparison verdict. There is no default promotion.

The [v2 platform revision](../../../docs/ace_role_revision.md) now addresses
local draft preservation, specific feedback, checked partial handoffs,
reviewed add/update/example decisions, prompt growth, durable budget-stop
recovery and cooperative pause. Its preflight used scripted responses and
real Rocq, with no paid calls. The separately authorized
[successor campaign](../ace_revision_20260913/README.md) measures LLM behavior
and book transfer. This document preserves the original v1 findings.

## What was implemented

- Small, role-specific instructions and executable demonstrations for
  curators, reducers and reflectors, shared by both agent harnesses.
- Short model-facing context/draft/receipt aliases, typed decisions and
  deterministic compilation into existing ACE operations. Immutable source
  identifiers remain in persisted evidence.
- Bounded, unassisted Rocq snippet checks with receipt reuse and successor
  contexts. Reducers consume actual curator outputs, including receipts;
  original drafts survive unsuccessful curation.
- A reflector lookup tool over recorded training events, authoritative
  terminal evidence, and a structured proposal/abstention contract.
- An independent $40 reservation ledger, development-only import/read
  guard, immutable manifests and code/input seal, exact HTTP-forbidden
  replay, real-Rocq receipt audits, and complete paired reporting.

The generator retains Luna-medium, core tools, the bounded/focused
controller, and the $0.10 / 64-request / 300-verifier-second limits.
The finalist changes its book. This isolates book transfer from adding
another runtime tool to every proof attempt.

## Pilot evidence

Both writer arms completed all eight curator and two reducer jobs validly.
The new interface retained **two** useful distinct corrections through the
reducer, versus **one** for receipt-v2, at **$0.02823912 versus $0.03525200**.
These are small, dependent source/batch observations, not an independent
theorem-level statistical test. The conservative source utility labels and
a more permissive sensitivity are in `pilot_review.json` and `audits/`.

All retained pilot snippets passed exact real-Rocq rechecks. Verification
alone was insufficient: `simpl in Hp` changed `1*y` into `y+0`, while `nia`
already closed the focused source branch without that step. The apparent
normalization was not credited as a useful new bridge. The source-local
sensitivity experiment is recorded, with no model calls.

The new reflector produced seven valid outputs out of eight, versus eight
for terminal-v2. Under the registered rubric it received five useful
reviews versus three; four of those five are specific justified
abstentions. Costs were **$0.05276304 versus $0.04374240**; cost per useful
review was within the registered limit. Neither arm was credited with an
unsupported execution claim. This is agent adjudication of arm-free item
exports, not independent human blinding; output format can reveal the arm.

The failed new-reflector job mixed proposed code with an abstention reason.
Its generic validation feedback incorrectly pointed to the event reference,
so the model repeated the conflict until the four-turn limit. The failed
outcome and all costs remain in the denominator. No repair or retry replaced
the paid measurement.

## Full adaptation exposes a different bottleneck

One pass over all 40 cached training trajectories produced 40 reflector,
34 curator and ten reducer jobs. Six abstentions skipped curation. The
reflector returned 39 valid outputs; all curators and reducers were valid.
Preparation cost **$0.31268266**. Eight entries survived into a 34-entry,
3994-token book, up from 26 entries and 2103 tokens.

Two additions are nonredundant reusable techniques: explicit nonzero-factor
elimination in a real polynomial proof, and natural power normalization
followed by exponent injectivity. Six additions repeat existing modulo,
arithmetic, recurrence, cast or closed-computation guidance. **All eight
come from already accepted training proofs; zero come from unsolved
sources.** The book was not manually pruned before evaluation.

This distinguishes the remaining failure mechanisms:

1. **Execution is not novelty.** A checked example can repeat an existing
   rule. The writer compiler checks evidence, identifiers and decision
   coverage; the lexical merge does not enforce semantic novelty. The
   reducer still retained six redundant entries.
2. **Review quality is not learning yield.** Correct abstention avoids
   invented evidence but contributes no new operation. Counting specific
   abstentions as useful was allowed by this pilot's rubric; it is a weak
   proxy for repairing unsolved sources. Preserve that gate result rather
   than retrospectively relabeling it as a failure or a coverage success.
3. **Accepted whole proofs are easy to package.** The new contract often
   turns successful trajectories into long, source-specific examples.
   Prefix checks certify those examples but do not establish cross-problem
   value. Meanwhile, partial repairs of difficult failures are more likely
   to be withheld or rejected by the role interface.
4. **Append-only advice leaves contradictions and duplicates.** Existing
   erroneous or overbroad advice is not automatically replaced by a more
   precise verified rule. More instructions can therefore increase prompt
   cost without repairing the originating failure.

## Which development ideas motivated the offline revision

The role-specific writer tools and deterministic bookkeeping are worth
keeping as opt-in infrastructure: the pilot shows concrete retention and
cost improvements. The next high-value work is a reducer that must identify
the closest existing rule and state its precise new information before
adding text, with an explicit path for replacing a faulty rule. Evaluate
this on useful retained corrections per token and repairs from unsolved
sources, then on complete paired proof panels.

For reflectors, separate “faithful diagnosis/abstention” from “new actionable
lesson” in the pilot metrics. A future contract should use an explicit
proposal-versus-abstention discriminant and field-specific error feedback.
It should permit clearly labeled unverified *local* repairs while requiring
writers to check them. An unfinished theorem is not evidence that every
successful prefix is unusable.

More generator tools are lower priority under the available evidence. The
previous development-only snippet campaign lost four validation solves and
increased inference cost by 32.9% against historical controls; its writing
arm produced no book change. Those observations do not prove that tools
are intrinsically unhelpful, but do not justify repeating the same treatment
before addressing the demonstrated writing/retention bottleneck. See the
adjacent snippet campaign's `FINDINGS.md` for its original denominators,
uncertainty, setup failures and unchanged verdicts.

Those changes are now available in the opt-in v2 contracts and regression
suite. Their subsequent measurements belong to the successor campaign.

These assessments use accessible development notes and measured artifacts,
not an unrestricted reread of mixed HINTS/history. The design preserves the
strategy/policy separation in [Delphyne](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05310)
and tests concrete role/handoff mechanisms inspired by
[ACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618). Neither paper substitutes for local
transfer evidence.

## Checks and limits

At the v1 milestone, thirty-one scoped tests passed. Pinned Pyright 1.1.406 passed on the changed
files, and Ruff passes. Root `make pyright` passes the core project but
still reports 17 pre-existing `why3py.simple` dependency/type errors in
`find_invariants`; no out-of-scope fix was made. Canonical instruction and
memory symlinks were checked without reading the mixed index.

Global tests, mixed reports, partition checks, aggregate repricing and
Ladon CLI status are excluded under the explicit data closure. The earlier
planning exposure to mixed historical snippets was disclosed and excluded
as analytical evidence. New experimental entry points enforce the closure;
only trainX and validationX are used. validationX is repeatedly reused
development data, even with fresh randomized paired controls. All API
confirmed charges are reconciled from dated token prices. Interrupted
requests retain their full unknown-charge reservation: $1.98787940 known
plus $2.12898800 held, or $4.11686740 maximum accounted. Codex-session billing
is unavailable and separate. See `platform_v2/quality_checks.json` for the
subsequent offline checks.
