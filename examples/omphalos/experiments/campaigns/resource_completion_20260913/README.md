## Diagnosis and treatments

The retrospective supports a budgeting trade-off more clearly than a general
ACE coverage benefit. Its current Luna book gains three validation solves
(27 versus 24, unadjusted p=.25), while the current no-book program regresses
against the earlier program. ACE cannot explain that program-level loss.
ValidationX is repeatedly reused development data.

Resource-control evidence motivates A: all 827 inspected trainX Luna calls
in the permitted attribution diagnostics used fewer than 4096 output tokens.
Five recorded terminal requests in the capacity no-book profile failed the
32768-output reservation but fit its 4096 counterpart. That is evidence of
admission conservatism, not proof that those requests would solve anything.
The earlier one-shot downshift had poor exposed-cell outcomes; this campaign
tests the allowance throughout, without also increasing request/time budgets.

The user selected corrected structured continuation as B. Its parser already
exists: duplicate final JSON messages are handled by the v2 interface. The
saved trainX `mathd_numbertheory_48` suffix reaches an assisted accepted proof.
We reuse that repair; the new campaign measures whether it actually reaches
useful dispatched requests in the current bounded program.

separate recorded amendment, preserving the paid prefix exactly once and

Training gates on correctness and actual exposure, not an effect-size or
p-value hurdle. A exposure requires a dispatched request whose shadow 32768
bound would fail, followed by an executed proof check. B exposure requires a
dispatched structured request followed by an executed assembled proof check.
An estimate, cached event or unexecuted proposal alone does not count.

An eligible validation arm qualifies for follow-up if it gains at least one
solve at cost ratio <=1.25, **or** loses at most one solve at ratio <=0.90.
Select by qualified solves, cost per solve, total cost, then A on an exact tie.
No extra work merely to cross a significance threshold.

After follow-up, retain an opt-in contender if the combined 80 cells gain at
least two solves at ratio <=1.25, or lose at most two at ratio <=0.90; fresh
seed 1 must not have both strictly lower coverage and strictly higher cost.
Otherwise reject retention under this gate. If no arm qualifies for follow-up,
stop after the initial comparison. Inconclusive evidence is not proof of no
benefit. No default promotion is authorized by any of these gates.

## Accounting, evaluation and failure rules

One SQLite ledger reserves before every HTTP attempt, across all processes.
SDK retries are disabled. Unknown charges retain full liabilities. This
campaign additionally stops admission atomically on unresolved billing,
transport errors needing reconciliation, or a violated bound. Other campaign
defaults remain unchanged. Every charge, including unsuccessful attempts,
appears in receipts and total cost. Price checks use dated registry tariffs.

Report qualified solve coverage, total spending and cost per qualified solve
together. A solve qualifies only if the verifier succeeds, the cell is not a
platform failure, and total cell charges are <=$0.10. Keep platform failures
in the denominator. Missing/administratively censored required cells or
unresolved required costs prohibit a verdict and stop further dispatch.

Use the existing family-clustered paired evaluator, clustering repeated seeds
with related theorem families. Support claims use two-sided p<.10 and 90%
intervals; Holm adjusts the two initial coverage claims even if an arm lacks
exposure. Follow-up inference is exploratory after selection. Practical gates,
statistical support and default promotion are distinct. Historical controls
are not randomized contemporary controls. Report cache fractions and
same-token uncached repricing alongside actual costs; such repricing is not
an experiment with another admission rule. Controller and runtime differences
are disclosed rather than absorbed into an ACE claim.

## Independent evidence repairs, no paid book creation

`ace/terminal_evidence.py` binds the submitted checker input, accepted script,
recorded assistance flag, source cell and cache/result/check hashes. The
returned outcome text already carried the accepted proof in legacy code.
The added receipt removes ambiguity about the terminal operation; its script
tails are explicitly a textual diff, not an execution trace. Formatting is
compared through the existing tactic splitter and both representations are
retained. Solved outcomes without a matching verifier receipt are rejected.

`ReflectOnTrajectoryV2` receives this receipt outside trajectory truncation.
`ACEAdaptStepConfig` opts in through version 2 and a pinned receipt hash,
using a distinct config name. Version 0 preserves historical queries.
Include `prove_evidence` in the execution context for the opt-in interface.

`CurateCheckedPlaybook` and `ReduceCheckedDeltas` carry explicit snippet
bindings. Their strategies call the existing `protect_reduction` through
Compute. The source witness is checked in its trainX environment; an invalid
rewrite is replaced by the verified source. Every incoming source must be
bound or explicitly dropped, and the returned bindings/verdicts must be
persisted before deterministic playbook merge. This is an explicit contract
for bound executable examples, not a claim that arbitrary prose is verified.
The offline evidence exporter persists curation and reduction receipts. None
of these changes affects this campaign's fixed book or launches an author.
